About me

I'm a data scientist from Ghana, currently in Kigali completing a master's in AI and Data Science at CMU Africa. Most of my work sits in computer vision: spotting people in search-and-rescue imagery, recognising faces, and comparing detection and segmentation models to see which ones actually earn their keep.

Lately I've been spending more time on what happens when models meet the real world. That means testing medical models against adversarial attacks, building tools that let non-technical people ask questions of their data, and writing about who is responsible when AI-written code goes wrong.

Outside the lab I look after the money for the CMU-Africa Data Science Club and represent Ghanaian students on campus.

Projects

Search-and-rescue person detection

A RetinaNet detector fine-tuned on the SARD dataset to find people in aerial rescue footage. The pipeline handles several annotation formats, uses augmentations suited to search-and-rescue scenes, trains with cosine annealing and is scored with COCO metrics.

  • RetinaNet
  • PyTorch
  • COCO evaluation
View on GitHub

Detection and segmentation, head to head

I compared YOLOv8, Faster R-CNN, EfficientDet and RetinaNet for detection, and SAM, CLIPSeg, U-Net, Mask R-CNN, DeepLabV3 and SegFormer for segmentation, on a dataset of bag images our group annotated. I traced and fixed a RetinaNet run that was returning zero predictions. The surprise: a U-Net trained from scratch beat the pretrained models, and classic Watershed beat them all.

  • YOLOv8
  • U-Net
  • SegFormer
  • SAM

Stress-testing medical AI

With three teammates, I'm adding adversarial attacks to medical data to see how far a model's predictions can be pushed. The hard part is working with a small dataset and being honest about what it can and can't tell us.

  • Adversarial attacks
  • Robustness

Phoneme recognition from speech

A frame-level phoneme classifier built only from fully connected layers, working on log-mel spectrograms under a 20 million parameter cap. No CNNs, RNNs, attention or pretrained weights allowed. Reached 87.6% validation accuracy.

  • MLP
  • Speech
  • PyTorch

Face classification and verification
